Project Governance · Balanced Ownership Model

Business-Led Requirements,
PM-Governed Delivery

The most successful projects combine deep domain knowledge from the business with disciplined structure from the PM. Neither party works in isolation — they play distinct, complementary roles at every stage.

Business owns the WHAT — content & accuracy
BA bridges the GAP — translation & structure
PM governs the HOW — process, quality & sign-off
The Model

Why Balance Beats Single Ownership

Assigning requirements entirely to either side creates blind spots. The balanced model assigns each role to the party best qualified to perform it.

🏢

Business Stakeholders own the content

They understand the process, the pain, and the expected outcome. They author requirements in their own language first — preserving domain accuracy.

🔄

BA translates and structures

The Business Analyst converts business intent into unambiguous, testable, traceable requirements — bridging domain knowledge and technical delivery.

📐

PM governs quality and process

The PM sets the template, enforces completeness, manages sign-off, and owns change control — without rewriting what the business said.

🤝

Shared accountability at gates

At each phase gate, both business and PM sign off — business for accuracy, PM for completeness and traceability. Neither can bypass the other.

Requirements Ownership Split — Balanced Model
🏢 Business & SMEs (Content & Accuracy — 43%)
🔄 BA (Structure — 28%)
📐 PM (Process & Quality — 29%)
Compare to PM-only: 100% PM burden · Business-only: no structure, no traceability
Lifecycle

Phase-by-Phase: Roles, Activities & Benefits

At each stage, the business and PM contribute differently — and the balanced model generates a specific, measurable benefit from that collaboration.

1
Mobilisation & Initiation
Project charter · governance setup · stakeholder mapping · risk register
PM: High
Business: Medium
🏢 Business Activities Contributor
  • ●Articulate strategic objectives and success criteria
  • ●Identify key business sponsors and decision-makers
  • ●Provide high-level scope boundaries and constraints
  • ●Confirm priority and urgency of the initiative
📐 PM Activities Governs
  • ●Draft and socialise the project charter
  • ●Establish steering committee and reporting cadence
  • ●Build initial RAID log; schedule risk workshops
  • ●Define requirements process, templates, and RACI
✨ Balanced Model Benefit

What this collaboration unlocks that single-ownership cannot:

Benefits at this stage
  • →Charter reflects real business priorities, not PM assumptions
  • →Governance is set up before conflict, not after
  • →Business feels ownership from day one — reducing resistance later
2
Requirements Gathering & Documentation
Discovery workshops · requirements authoring · BA structuring · PM sign-off process
PM: High
Business: Very High
🏢 Business Activities Authors Content
  • ●Lead requirements workshops as the domain expert
  • ●Draft user stories and process flows in their own language
  • ●Prioritise requirements (MoSCoW) with business rationale
  • ●Review and confirm BA-structured requirements for accuracy
  • ●Formally sign off on the baselined requirements document
📐 PM Activities Governs Quality
  • ●Facilitate workshops (agenda, timing, outputs)
  • ●Enforce completeness against the requirements template
  • ●Identify gaps in non-functional requirements
  • ●Manage sign-off process and version control
  • ●Set up change request process before baseline is locked
✨ Balanced Model Benefit

The most impactful phase for the balanced model:

Benefits at this stage
  • →Requirements carry domain authority — business wrote them
  • →Structure and traceability ensured by BA + PM — no gaps
  • →Business ownership drives faster, more decisive sign-off
  • →Fewer change requests during build as intent is preserved
3
Solution Design
Functional & technical design · architecture · prototype review
PM: Medium
Business: Medium
🏢 Business Activities Validator
  • ●Review functional design against their authored requirements
  • ●Participate in prototype walkthroughs and provide feedback
  • ●Confirm that design decisions match business intent
  • ●Flag any design departures from agreed requirements
📐 PM Activities Tracks & Governs
  • ●Manage design review sessions and track open items
  • ●Maintain traceability from requirements to design decisions
  • ●Assess impact of any design changes on scope and timeline
  • ●Obtain formal design sign-off from business and tech leads
✨ Balanced Model Benefit
Benefits at this stage
  • →Business can validate design against requirements they wrote — reducing misinterpretation
  • →Design iterations are fewer because intent was clear from the start
  • →PM's traceability matrix catches drift early
4
Build & Development
Configuration · coding · unit testing · change request management
PM: Medium
Business: Low
🏢 Business Activities On-Call SME
  • ●Respond promptly to BA/dev clarification queries
  • ●Review and approve any change requests within SLA
  • ●Begin preparing end-user test scenarios for UAT
📐 PM Activities Controls & Protects
  • ●Track build progress against plan; manage risks daily
  • ●Triage and route clarification queries efficiently
  • ●Gate all change requests — reject gold-plating
  • ●Report status to steering committee
✨ Balanced Model Benefit
Benefits at this stage
  • →Business disruption is minimised — low effort, SME-only queries
  • →Clarifications resolved faster because business authored requirements — no telephone game
  • →PM blocks scope creep while keeping business engaged
5
Testing — SIT & UAT
Test planning · system integration testing · user acceptance testing · defect triage
PM: High
Business: Very High
🏢 Business Activities Leads UAT
  • ●Contribute to UAT test scripts based on their own requirements
  • ●Execute UAT with full domain context — not just button-clicking
  • ●Classify defects: blocking vs. cosmetic vs. enhancement
  • ●Formally sign off UAT once exit criteria are met
📐 PM Activities Coordinates & Gates
  • ●Oversee test planning; ensure traceability to requirements
  • ●Coordinate test environments and data
  • ●Run defect triage; enforce severity classification
  • ●Track UAT progress against go-live readiness criteria
✨ Balanced Model Benefit
Benefits at this stage
  • →UAT defect rate is lower — business tested against requirements they authored
  • →UAT sign-off is faster and more confident — no "I didn't mean that"
  • →Disputes about scope are resolved by the requirements baseline, not opinions
6
Go-Live & Deployment
Cutover planning · training · communications · production launch
PM: Very High
Business: High
🏢 Business Activities Champion Change
  • ●Drive end-user change management and adoption
  • ●Lead or participate in training delivery
  • ●Provide go/no-go input from a business readiness perspective
  • ●Communicate launch to operational teams
📐 PM Activities Executes Cutover
  • ●Own and execute the cutover plan
  • ●Manage go/no-go meeting and decision log
  • ●Coordinate deployment with tech and infrastructure teams
  • ●Stand up incident management for hypercare
✨ Balanced Model Benefit
Benefits at this stage
  • →Business champions adoption — they built what they asked for
  • →Go-live is a shared milestone, not a handover from PM to business
  • →Resistance to change is lower when business shaped the solution
7
Hypercare & Project Closure
Live issue management · BAU handover · lessons learned · benefit realisation
PM: Medium
Business: Medium
🏢 Business Activities Takes Ownership
  • ●Triage and prioritise live operational issues
  • ●Accept formal BAU handover from the project team
  • ●Contribute to lessons learned from a business perspective
  • ●Baseline benefit realisation metrics for future tracking
📐 PM Activities Closes & Learns
  • ●Monitor hypercare period; manage SLA for live issues
  • ●Facilitate lessons learned workshop
  • ●Produce project closure report and benefit realisation plan
  • ●Formally close project and release team
✨ Balanced Model Benefit
Benefits at this stage
  • →Business takes BAU ownership confidently — they were involved throughout
  • →Lessons learned are richer — both parties reflect on shared accountability
  • →Benefit realisation is more achievable — solution matches what was actually needed

Summary

What the Balanced Model Delivers

Across the full lifecycle, shared ownership produces outcomes that neither party achieves alone.

🎯

Higher Requirement Accuracy

Business authors requirements in their own language — domain knowledge is preserved, not filtered through a PM layer.

📐

Structural Rigour

PM and BA ensure completeness, non-functional coverage, and traceability — structure the business alone would not produce.

⚡

Faster Sign-Offs

Business signs off on what they wrote. No re-explanation cycle. Fewer escalations and "this isn't what I meant" moments.

🔒

Shared Accountability

Dual sign-off at every gate means no single party can deflect. Disputes are resolved by the baselined document, not politics.

📉

Lower UAT Defect Rate

Business UAT against their own requirements surfaces real-world gaps rather than testing against a PM's interpretation.

🚀

Stronger BAU Adoption

Business was a co-owner throughout. At go-live, they champion the solution rather than receive it.
